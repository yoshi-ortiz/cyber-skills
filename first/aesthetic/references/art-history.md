# Art history

Art history supplies contextual precedent: who made something, for whom, under
which material, political, religious, technological, and institutional
conditions, and how later viewers interpreted it. University curricula require
chronological, geographic, and methodological breadth rather than a parade of
visual styles.[^yale-history-of-art]

## Use lineage, not costume

- Identify the historical relationship being borrowed: production method,
  distribution model, spatial logic, typographic system, political program,
  material constraint, or reception—not just surface texture.
- Check chronology, technique, interpretation, and context against a substantive
  academic course or institutional reference.[^open-university-art-history]
- Name discontinuities. A contemporary interface using Constructivist diagonals
  does not thereby inherit Constructivism's politics or material conditions.
- Avoid collapsing cultures, regions, or centuries into one aesthetic label.
- Treat movement names as research prompts and precedents, never presets.

## Application to art direction

A historical citation earns its place when it explains an observable decision
and its limits. State the precedent, the relationship being adapted, the
context that does not transfer, and the corpus or user evidence that makes the
adaptation relevant now. Historical authority cannot substitute for user
sentiment.
