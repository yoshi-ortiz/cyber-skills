# Kano and UX prioritization

Kano's original work treats quality through fulfillment and satisfaction and
uses questionnaire responses to classify product-quality elements. The central
categories are must-be, one-dimensional (performance), and attractive. In
practice, teams commonly preserve indifferent, reverse, and questionable
responses too. Ask paired functional (present) and dysfunctional (absent)
questions for one independently understandable feature proposition, then retain
the response pair, population, context, and wording with the classification.

## Economics purpose

Use the observed satisfaction category as one input to allocating a finite
discovery or delivery budget: protect must-be failures, compare investment in
performance needs, and test attractive hypotheses. The attached PMI reading
usefully connects the method to funding and risk choices under scarce resources;
that is practitioner guidance, not a predictive economic model.

Kano does not determine cost, revenue, adoption, market size, feasibility,
strategic fit, or ROI. Pair it with those separate evidence streams before
making an investment decision, and repeat the study when expectations or the
target population change.

## Relation to UX

Kano is a UX/product-research prioritization method for explicit feature
propositions. It complements generative research about needs and context,
usability and accessibility research, and the research-derived summaries held
in personas or archetypes. It neither creates a persona nor validates a user
segment, behavior, or representation.

## Boundary

Survey output records stated conditional satisfaction for the sampled population
and question framing. It is not objective value, a causal result, an
implementation directive, or a substitute for observing use. Do not use category
names as fixed market facts: expectations can change as features become common.
