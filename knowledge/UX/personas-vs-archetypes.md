# Personas vs. archetypes

Personas and archetypes summarize the same kinds of research-derived audience
clusters: behaviors, attitudes, motivations, pain points, and goals. They are
not demographic or personality segmentation. An archetype uses an abstract
behavioral or attitudinal label; a persona presents that same core user type as
a plausible named person with a face, biography, or narrative.

## UX purpose

Use either form to make user research usable in product decisions. Archetypes
offer a concise, less-personified shared frame. Personas can make a small,
coherent set of research findings easier to remember and discuss, and may invite
empathy. Names, portraits, and biographies are communication devices; only the
research-grounded behavioral claims should drive a decision.

Trace each cluster to the underlying research, clustering rationale, target
context, and observed attributes. Mark which details materially affect a
decision. Refresh the representation when new research changes the evidence.

## Boundary

Do not substitute marketing-only demographic or brand-affinity profiles for UX
evidence, or invent irrelevant biographical detail. One face necessarily reduces
a diverse group and can exclude people; choosing diverse avatars alone is not
inclusive design. Neither representation replaces usability testing, continued
research, or validation with people in the relevant context.
